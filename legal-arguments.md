Yes, very similar in principle. Copyright disputes in music often turn on the distinction between a protected creative choice and musical elements that are commonplace, constrained, or independently created.

Our constellation problem has an especially strong constraint: the stars are already in fixed positions. If we independently decide to use only bright stars and make Cygnus resemble a swan, the number of sensible line arrangements may become fairly small. Similarity can therefore arise without copying.

The useful copyright concept here is sometimes called the merger doctrine: when an idea can be expressed in only a limited number of practical ways, copyright protection for that particular expression can be correspondingly narrow. There is also the related concept of scènes à faire, for elements that naturally follow from the subject or convention.

So yes—the music analogy is useful, although the legal details differ between musical compositions and constellation drawings.
